## Verification

We reviewed 10 recordings in Label Studio, comparing annotators' labels, checking class assignments and onset/offset precision, enforcing the one-second pause rule, 
and flagging missed events. Incorrect annotations were corrected where possible; ambiguous cases were commented on and accepted.

**Main disagreements:**
- Most were about temporal boundaries (onsets and offsets), often involving violations of the one-second rule.
- Common causes were short or faint sounds, heavily overlapping events, and transitions between similar sources (e.g., toilet refilling vs. running water).
- Some audible target classes were neither annotated nor listed in the metadata. In file 003679, for example, footsteps were audible throughout but missing from both.

## Annotator Agreement

- **Overall:** mean Cohen's kappa of **0.788** across 5,735 annotator pairs, which is substantial agreement on the Landis & Koch scale.
- **Lowest:** `footsteps` (0.770), `door_open_close` (0.839), `light_switch` (0.898), all short events with ambiguous boundaries.
- **Highest:** `coffee_machine` (0.983), `vacuum_cleaner` (0.981), `bell_ringing` (0.979), all sustained and acoustically distinct.
- **Recording owner effect:** pairs involving the owner agree slightly more (κ = 0.792 vs. 0.776).
- **Metadata match:** all uploader-tagged classes appear in the annotations in 85.6% of files.

## Label Construction

Each annotator's overlap value is binarized at 0.5, then labels are decided by majority vote across annotators (≥ 0.5). With two annotators, 
ties resolve as positive, which slightly biases labels toward "present". Shared annotator errors are preserved.

## Dataset Characteristics

- **Size:** 168,239 segments (~46.7 hours).
- **Class imbalance:** `footsteps` is the most frequent class (1,815 files, 15.3% of total time) and `bell_ringing` the rarest (276 files, 1.5%).
- **Durations:** sustained sounds are long (`vacuum_cleaner` median 32 s), while transients are short (`light_switch` 2 s).
- **Features:** 960 dimensions (14 base features × 4 aggregations). Scales vary widely, so standardization is essential.
- **Feature profiles:** `vacuum_cleaner` has the highest energy, `running_water` the highest `zcr` and `flatness`, and `microwave` a low `centroid`. `footsteps` and `keyboard_typing` look alike and are hard to separate.
- **t-SNE:** sustained sounds (`vacuum_cleaner`, `running_water`) form clear clusters, while transients (`footsteps`, `light_switch`) overlap diffusely.

## Biases and Limitations

1. **Device bias:** a few smartphone models dominate, so classifiers may learn device characteristics.
2. **Environment bias:** classes are tied to rooms (e.g., `running_water` to kitchens), so models may learn room acoustics.
3. **Annotator bias:** with mostly two annotators per file, systematic errors flow directly into the labels.

## Conclusion

The dataset is suitable for training sound event detectors, but it needs handling for class imbalance, feature normalization, and label noise near the boundaries of transient events. 
Because it is a domestic sound dataset, the license should explicitly prohibit surveillance use.
